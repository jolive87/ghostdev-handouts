It takes the meter files we already produce, learns what normal looks like for every meter, tells you in plain English when something breaks, and turns that into a bill.

Where a metering operation loses money

LeakWhy it's expensive
A meter goes downNobody knows. It reads zero or reads wrong until somebody notices on an invoice
A bill doesn't go outThe reading never made it. That's revenue that simply doesn't happen
Rates changeNobody found out. We keep billing the old number
Tariffs changeThe same thing, but with bigger consequences — and it's ConEd's document, not ours

Every one of these is invisible until it costs money. That is the entire problem this attacks.

What works today

  • Ingests our real meter export files — a genuine 85-column master file: 4,818 rows, zero errors.
  • Learns every meter's normal and flags deviations, with false alerts driven down to 0.0033 per meter per day while still catching injected real faults 8 out of 8.
  • Turns anomalies into assigned, tracked work.
  • Produces an itemized bill preview — energy, demand, fixed, total — on flat and time-of-use structures, with demand computed the way ConEd actually bills it.
  • Multi-tenant, with hard isolation between clients.

What I need from you to price it

  • How many buildings, and how many metered points each
  • How many meters are already on a call-up system today
  • What's installed out there — make, model, age
  • Which sites have a network path out and which would need cellular
  • Whether CT ratios are documented anywhere reliable, or whether that's a survey
  • Retention requirement — how far back billing needs to reach
  • What this has to coexist with or replace

Give me that and I'll come back with a real scope and a real number based on your buildings instead of my assumptions.

What it doesn't do yet — stated plainly

CT/PT ratios not applied to financial figures. Rate schedules on screen are samples. LL97 view covers electrical emissions only and must not be projected past the current compliance period. Three of six detectors need channels most installed meters don't report. There is no AI generating any number you see — every figure is computed and auditable.

How the billing module gets built

I'm not going to tell your billing department how it should work. You tell me how the bills should come out, what the department needs to see, and what tenants get — and that's what gets built. The foundation is already there; what goes on top is your call.
