Where a metering operation loses money

LeakWhy it's expensive
A meter goes downNobody knows. It reads zero or reads wrong until somebody notices on an invoice
A bill doesn't go outThe reading never made it. That's revenue that simply doesn't happen
Rates changeNobody found out. We keep billing the old number
Tariffs changeThe same thing, but with bigger consequences — and it's ConEd's document, not ours

Every one of these is invisible until it costs money. That is the entire problem this attacks.

What works today

  • Ingests our real meter export files — a genuine 85-column master file: 4,818 rows, zero errors.
  • Learns every meter's normal and flags deviations, with false alerts driven down to 0.0033 per meter per day while still catching injected real faults 8 out of 8.
  • Turns anomalies into assigned, tracked work.
  • Produces an itemized bill preview — energy, demand, fixed, total — on flat and time-of-use structures, with demand computed the way ConEd actually bills it.
  • Multi-tenant, with hard isolation between clients.

What it costs to find out

Phase 1 — the pilot

Zero hardware. Zero installs. One folder of files. One building, 90 days of the meter exports we already produce. The system builds its baselines and we compare what it catches against what we catch by hand. If it finds one power factor penalty or one failing meter before we do, it has paid for itself.

Turning the pilot into real numbers

Two things close the gap between what you saw and a bill you could run on: one real ConEd bill to replace the sample rates, and the CT/PT ratios per meter — already on the meter cards, a field survey, not a purchase. A photocopy and a walk-through.

Past the pilot there's a full build-out — a local AI system trained on your operation, on hardware you own, handling the document and data work your people shouldn't be doing.
