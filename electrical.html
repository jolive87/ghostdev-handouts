Electrical Engineering

What it measures, how it decides something is wrong, and every tariff number with its source.

What it reads

ChannelAvailability in the field
kWh, kW demand, phase currents, system PFEssentially universal — including the E-Mon D-Mon Class 2000, the most common installed sub-meter
kVAR, THD, per-phase voltageAbsent on an estimated 85–90% of installed NYC sub-meters. The platform carries a meter capability model, so on those meters the relevant detectors report "this meter cannot measure that" rather than a green all-clear

That distinction matters more than it sounds. A "no problems found" on a channel the meter never measured is worse than no answer at all.

Baselines

  • One baseline per meter — two identical meters on the same floor legitimately have different normals.
  • Bucketed by hour of day × day of week, in New York local time (this was a real defect once: UTC bucketing shifted every bucket 4–5 hours and the shift moved at DST).
  • 90 days minimum. Below that, the meter is not analyzed at all.

Detection

  • Six engines: voltage deviation, demand spike, zero reading, power-factor drift, THD, and cross-meter correlation — plus a current-unbalance detector, which per NEMA MG-1 is the more sensitive indicator and one of the few channels real meters actually report
  • Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate control. Measured on pure noise: 13.1 false positives per meter per day → 0.0033, while injected real faults were still caught 8 of 8.
  • Voltage unbalance uses the NEMA MG-1 definition — maximum deviation from average, divided by average. The common (max−min)/avg form overstates it by 1.5–2×.
  • Power factor is aggregated as ΣkW / ΣkVA, not a mean of ratios. The mean-of-ratios form is biased high, which understates the exact penalty it's meant to price.
  • Related anomalies group into events: one 2-hour incident went from 104 alerts down to 13.

On the demand ratchet

Earlier versions of this project carried a rule stating that billing demand is the greater of this month's peak or 80% of the highest peak in the prior 12 months. An exhaustive search of the filed tariff — every General Rule and Service Classification in PSC No. 10 — returns zero occurrences of "ratchet", "80%", "eighty percent", or "highest demand established". SC-9 has no "Billing Demand" heading at all.

It had been copied from a generic list of utility mechanisms. It was removed, and a regression test now fails the build if anyone reintroduces it. What actually exists is the 18-month Contract Demand window above: longer memory, much smaller bite.

Compliance

LL84 / LL97 tracking is electrical emissions only, stated on every screen. A building burning gas can look compliant here and be over its limit in reality. Multi-fuel is roadmap, not shipped.
